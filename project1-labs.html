In this lab, you will start learning about soundwalks by listening at soundwalks made by students from last year. These soundwalks invite you to listen closely and discover how the city's past and present continue to interact in the spaces you inhabit today.

mdi-targetLearning Objectives

  • Understand the concept and purpose of soundwalks.
  • Experience a soundwalk using the Echoes app.
  • Reflect on auditory experiences and space perception.

mdi-toolsTools You'll Use

  • Headphones or earbuds
  • Your smartphone (iOS or Android)

mdi-walkWhat is a Soundwalk?

A soundwalk is an exploration of the environment through listening. Participants move through a space, focusing on sounds both natural and human-made. Learn more here.

mdi-numeric-1-circleStep 1: Install the Echoes App

Get started by installing the Echoes app from: Google Play, or Apple App Store

mdi-numeric-2-circleStep 2: Access a Soundwalk

  1. Explore the following page with soundwalks from last year's GBDA students: https://gbdawu.github.io/gbda412/soundwalk-exhibition
  2. Find one soundwalk you like and scan the QR code to access the soundwalk in the Echoes app

mdi-numeric-3-circleStep 3: Follow the Map in the App

Once your soundwalk is loaded, go out of the building and follow the map in the app to trigger audios at various locations near Stratford School.

mdi-numeric-4-circleStep 4: Complete and Discuss

Once you have completed the soundwalk, discuss your experience with the class:

  • What did you hear?
  • How did the sounds make you feel?
  • What surprised you about the environment?
  • What did you learn about the space you were in?

mdi-cellphoneStream 2: Record Sounds With Your Cellphone or Field Recorder
mdi-bullseye-arrow Objective: Capture sounds in Stratford to provide a current perspective on your theme. You can record sounds using cellphone or Zoom H6 field recorders.
  1. Choose and go to a location near Stratford School to record an audio based on the theme you selected in Step 1. Follow the steps below based on your recording device:

    • If you choose to record with your cellphone:
      • Use your phone's recorder to capture at least two to three different sounds related to your theme. Keep each recording short (1 - 2 minutes long).
      • Follow these tips for good recordings:
        • Hold your phone steady and close to the sound source.
        • Avoid covering the microphone.
        • Minimize background noise (wind, traffic, etc.).
        • Record multiple takes if needed.
        Example
        Record near the entrance door of Stratford School. Capture layers of everyday sound such as footsteps, conversations, and doors opening.

        How this example connects to readings from Weeks 1-4:
        • Pattern of time: Notice how rhythms of arrival and departure mark the passage of time on campus. Consider whether certain voices or activities repeat daily, creating recognizable temporal patterns.
        • Sense of place and belonging: Reflect on how overlapping sounds form a shared soundscape, shaping feelings of community or exclusion in this space.
      • Back in the classroom, transfer your recordings to your computer (e.g., email them to yourself, use AirDrop, Google Drive, or OneDrive)
      • Rename your files descriptively (e.g., sounds_from_campus_entrance_door).
      • If your recordings are not already in .mp3 or .wav format, convert them using a tool like CloudConvert. These format are compatible with a range of devices from cellphones to laptops.

    • If you choose to record with the Zoom H6 field recorder:
      • Familiarize with the Zoom H6 field recorder:
      • Before going out to record:
        • Check that the batteries and SD card are inserted and that you have fresh batteries.
        • Choose the microphone capsule (X/Y or shotgun) based on your sound source (e.g., use the X/Y capsule for capturing the overall ambience of a busy hallway, or the shotgun capsule for focusing on a single speaker/source at a distance).
        • PressLR buttons to turn on the microphone.
        • Press Record ⏺ once to start recording (the red light will be steady).
        • Press Play ▶ to listen to your recording.Use ⏮ or ⏭ to skip to other recordings.
        • When finished, stop the recording and power off before removing the SD card.
      • Go out to capture two to three different sounds related to your theme. Keep each recording short (1 - 2 minutes long).
        • Follow these tips for good recordings:
          • Hold your phone steady and close to the sound source.
          • Avoid covering the microphone.
          • Minimize background noise (wind, traffic, etc.).
          • Record multiple takes if needed.
        Example
        Use the shotgun microphone to focus on a single sound source, such as a street performer near Stratford's City Hall. Capture the performer's voice or instrument in detail while reducing background noise from passing cars or conversations.

        How this example connects to readings from Weeks 1-4:
        • Bodies and spaces: Reflect on how isolating one sound changes the narrative of the soundscape. What story emerges when we privilege an individual performer's sound over the surrounding environment?
        • Narratives of daily life: Consider how this emphasis highlights cultural or social meanings connected to music and public space, and how listening practices shape collective experience.
      • Back in the classroom, transfer your files via the SD card reader or USB cable.
      • Rename your files descriptively (e.g., performer_near_city_hall).
      • Once you have your recorded narration file ready, proceed to Step 2 to import, layer with other sounds, edit, and export your final audio mixdown.

mdi-account-voice Stream 3: Voice Annotations or Narrations
mdi-bullseye-arrow Objective: Create audio based on your own narration and reflections. You can:
  • Develop creative texts (e.g., stories, poems, reflections), or
  • Use factual texts (e.g., historical notes, news excerpts)
  1. Choose a location or space at Stratford that connects to your soundwalk theme.
  2. Write a short script (2–3 sentences) describing your experience, observations, or reflections on that space.
    • Your script can be creative (e.g., a poem or story) or factual (e.g., historical information or accessibility details).
      • For factual or historical information, explore the Streets of Stratford website.
    • Link your script to themes from the readings (e.g., environment, place and belonging, bodies and spaces, gender and culture, etc.).
    Example
    Start by exploring St. Patrick Street near Stratford School. Research its history on the Streets of Stratford website. Create 2–3 short audio narration notes (60–90 seconds each) that highlight historical facts, everyday observations, and personal reflections to tell a story of everyday life, past and present.

    How this example connects to readings from Weeks 1–4:
    • Environmental change: Consider how the soundscape has evolved over time, from historical events to the current everyday atmosphere of the street.
    • Social and cultural meanings: Think about how certain sounds, spaces, or activities may carry historical, cultural, or gendered significance.
  3. Record Your Voice Narration:
    • Use your computer's microphone, phone recorder, or a field recorder to read and capture your written script aloud.
    • Aim for a clear recording with minimal background disturbance/noise. Take a few tries if necessary.
    • Transfer or save your raw voice recording file to your computer and rename it descriptively (e.g., st_patrick_street_narration.wav).
    • Once you have your recorded narration file ready, proceed to Step 2 to import, layer with other sounds, edit, and export your final audio mixdown.

mdi-tune-vertical Step 2: Edit Your Audio Recording

mdi-bullseye-arrow Objective:

Learn how to use BandLab to import, arrange, trim, layer, and export your soundwalk or field recording audio files cleanly.

  • Create a BandLab Account and Start a Project
    💡 Tip: BandLab is a free, browser-based digital audio workstation that makes it easy to multi-track, layer, and edit audio files for your soundwalk.
    • Go to https://www.bandlab.com/
    • Click Sign Up to create your free account using your school or personal email.
    • Once logged in, click Create > New Project to open a new project workspace.
  • Import Your Media
    • Click on Drop or drag a loop or audio and select the file to import.
    • Wait for the file to upload and load onto your workspace timeline.
    • Ensure your track is placed on the main timeline workspace to start editing.
  • Edit Your Audio
    • Trim and Split the audio: Position the white playhead where you want to cut and press 'S' to cut unwanted portions, and delete sections you don't need.
    • Layer multiple sounds: Import additional field recordings or archival files onto new tracks to build your sound collage.
    • Add fade effects: Hover over the edges of your audio clips to drag and create smooth Fade In and Fade Out transitions.
  • Export Your Audio
    • Click the hamburger menu in the top right corner of the Mix Editor. Then click Project > Download > Mixdown As.
    • Choose MP3.
    • Name your file and confirm the download.
    • Save your finished audio file to your computer.
