GBDA 412 - Audio Cultures

Group Project 1: The Politics of Sound and Place (35% total)

Individually, you will create a soundwalk for a site or route at Stratford that explores the politics of sound and place. Drawing on concepts and discussions from Weeks 1 to 4, your soundwalk will invite listeners to explore the meanings, identities, power relations, and everyday experiences associated with Stratford.

What is a soundwalk? A soundwalk is a form of active listening that focuses on the acoustic environment of a specific location or route. Participants walk through a space while paying particular attention to the sounds around them. Learn more

Overall Learning Outcomes

By completing this project, students will:

  1. Create site-specific sound art: Develop technical proficiency in field recording, audio editing, and location-based technology to produce original artwork that reveals new perspectives on familiar spaces.
  2. Connect artistic practice to theoretical frameworks: Analyze how sound-based art can illuminate theoretical ideas about space, sound, and everyday experience.
  3. Reflect critically on creative learning: Articulate creative decisions, evaluate different artistic approaches, and analyze personal development throughout the project process using clear written and oral communication.

Artist Statement Requirements

500 words addressing the following points:

  • The central theme, story, or sonic argument of the composition. Explain why this specific site or route was chosen and what or acoustic experience you are creating for the listener.
  • ituate the work within the broader discourse of acoustic ecology, sound art, or sound studies. Draw connections to key concepts from Weeks 1–4 to ground the creative practice in theory.
  • Describe the nature and origin of your source materials , including field recordings, archival samples, or ambient acoustics, and explain how these serve the narrative.
  • Explain other structural and technical choices (e.g., panning, layering, site-specificity, looping, rhythm, contrast) used guide the listener's physical journey through the environment.

Additional Tips on Audio Recodings

Reflect how can you create complex or layered audio recordings that get the audience immersed (i.e., involved deeply) in the space? Ask yourself:

  • Do your sounds stand out from the live environment? Do they guide the listener’s attention?
  • Which unique sounds or locations can help express your theme better?
  • Are there narrative choices that will make your soundwalk memorable?
  • What other connections to course readings can enhance your project?
  • If your audio recordings blend in too much, consider layering multiple sounds or adding narration using your voice or text-to-speech to create a richer experience.
    • You can layer sound in multiple tracks using Clipchamp (consult Workshop #2 to learn how)

    What You Need to Do

    Each soundwalk will be accessible to the class. Students will explore different soundwalks around Strarford School of Interaction and provide constructive oral feedback to their peers. The entire class period is dedicated to this exhibition activity.

    Learning Objectives

    • Experience and critically engage with peer-created sound-based artworks
    • Develop skills in providing constructive feedback on creative work
    • Practice active listening and spatial awareness through soundwalk participation
    • Learn to articulate responses to artistic work in oral discussion

    Exhibition Structure

    First hour: Experience 2+ different soundwalks around campus

    Second hour: Classroom feedback session

    What to Bring

    • Smartphone with Echoes app
    • Headphones/earbuds
    • Notebook to take notes about the soundwalk

    Exhibition participation:

    • Be present for the full 2 hours of class
    • Experience at least 2 different soundwalks created by your peers
    • Select soundwalks based on sites you are comfortable accessing (all locations are at a walking distance from Stratford School of Interaction campus to ensure accessibility)
    • Actively engage by listening thoughtfully and taking notes for feedback

    Feedback requirements:

    After experiencing each soundwalk, provide constructive oral feedback to the presenting team focusing on:
    • Specific observations about what worked well
    • Concrete suggestions for improvement
    • Questions about creative choices or technical decisions
    • Connections to course themes and concepts

    Example Feedback Prompts

    Use these prompts to guide your constructive feedback:
    • What aspects of the soundwalk did you find most engaging or thought-provoking?
    • How did the sound selection contribute to your experience of the site?
    • Were the transitions between sounds smooth? Did they create a cohesive journey?
    • How did the soundwalk make you think differently about this space? (Even if the change feels small, try to notice new details, perspectives, or feelings you may not have considered before.)
    • What specific improvements or adjustments would enhance the experience?
    • How effectively did the work connect to course themes from weeks 1-4?

      • Any creative innovations that impressed or inspired you
      • What these comparisons taught you about sound-based art
      • What you found effective or less effective in other teams' work
      • How their approaches influenced your thinking about soundwalk creation
