In this lab, you will start learning about soundwalks by listening at soundwalks made by students from last year. These soundwalks invite you to listen closely and discover how the city's past and present continue to interact in the spaces you inhabit today.

mdi-targetLearning Objectives

  • Understand the concept and purpose of soundwalks.
  • Experience a soundwalk using the Echoes app.
  • Reflect on auditory experiences and space perception.

mdi-toolsTools You'll Use

  • Headphones or earbuds
  • Your smartphone (iOS or Android)

mdi-walkWhat is a Soundwalk?

A soundwalk is an exploration of the environment through listening. Participants move through a space, focusing on sounds both natural and human-made. Learn more here.

mdi-numeric-1-circleStep 1: Install the Echoes App

Get started by installing the Echoes app from: Google Play, or Apple App Store

mdi-numeric-2-circleStep 2: Access a Soundwalk

  1. Explore the following page with soundwalks from last year's GBDA students: https://gbdawu.github.io/gbda412/soundwalk-exhibition
  2. Find one soundwalk you like and scan the QR code to access the soundwalk in the Echoes app

mdi-numeric-3-circleStep 3: Follow the Map in the App

Once your soundwalk is loaded, go out of the building and follow the map in the app to trigger audios at various locations near Stratford School.

mdi-numeric-4-circleStep 4: Complete and Discuss

Once you have completed the soundwalk, discuss your experience with the class:

  • What did you hear?
  • How did the sounds make you feel?
  • What surprised you about the environment?
  • What did you learn about the space you were in?

Homework: Theme Ideation

Due Before Next Class • Conceptual Prep for Lab 2

Before our next lab session, ideate a theme for your soundwalk project that critically engages with the politics of sound and place at Stratford School and its immediate surroundings, drawing connections to our readings from Weeks 1 through 4.

mdi-target Objective Select and articulate a theme exploring how auditory experiences shape spatial understanding, power dynamics, community, or identity in and around campus.

mdi-lightbulb-on-outline Prompts for Reflection:

mdi-headphone-dot
  • In what ways can walking and listening create a compelling story or narrative of daily life on and around campus?
  • How do everyday sounds around Stratford shape your personal sense of place , inclusion, or alienation?
  • What specific sounds are associated with different bodies , social groups, or activities near Stratford School?
  • Are there s ounds or spaces that carry gendered, cultural, economic, or social meanings in the city of Stratford?
  • How do rhythms, routines, or repetitions of sound reveal underlying patterns of time in Stratford's everyday environment?
mdi-notebook-edit-outline Your Submission Deliverable:

Next class, bring 1-2 sentences linking your proposed soundwalk to at least one key concept or reading from Weeks 1-4. Keep it flexible, as your theme will naturally evolve when we gather audio recordings next class.

{{ labs[1].title }}

This lab will guide you through collecting and editing audio to give you ideas for your soundwalk recordings. By the end, you'll have hands-on experience with audio capture and editing techniques.

mdi-targetLearning Objectives

  • Identify and extract sounds from archival media.
  • Capture your own recordings of campus life.
  • Layer recordings with narration or text-to-speech.

mdi-toolsTools You'll Use

  • Headphones or earbuds
  • Sounds of Stratford website, Stratford School YouTube channel, Stratford City YouTube Channel
  • Your smartphone (iOS or Android)
  • Clipchamp (clipchamp.com)

mdi-lightbulbStep 1: Start with a Theme

Objective: Refine or reimagine your theme of everyday life at Stratford School and its surroundings. Your theme will guide your sound collection, editing, and connection to course readings.
  1. Revisit your proposal from last week. Remind yourself of the theme(s) you first imagined for your soundwalk. You can also see what other people are planning by reading the proposals on the discussion forum on LEARN.
  2. Reflect on how your theme can connect with ideas from Weeks 1–4. For example:
    • In what ways can walking and listening create a story or narrative of daily life on and around campus?
    • How do everyday sounds around Stratford shape your sense of place and belonging?
    • What sounds are associated with different bodies and spaces near Stratford School?
    • Are there sounds or places that carry gendered, cultural, or social meanings in the context of Stratford School and its surroundings?
    • How do rhythms, routines, or repetitions of sound reveal patterns of time in Stratford’s everyday environment?
  3. Write down a refined theme statement (1-2 sentences). Keep it flexible — your theme can evolve as you gather new sounds and ideas. If you haven't submitted a proposal yet, use this step to start your initial theme proposal (consult guideline on LEARN).

mdi-microphoneStep 2: Collect Sound Recordings

Soundwalks can include many different types of recordings. For your project, you'll get to choose between using existing audio from archival media or creating your own recordings with your cellphone or field recorder or even text-to-speech tools.

For today's lab, start by exploring one of the sound collection streams below. If you finish one before class ends, try another! If you only get to one, that's fine too — you can always explore the others at home.

mdi-archiveStream 1: Collect Existing Audio Recordings
Objective: Identify and collect existing audio from archival media that reflect everyday life at Stratford School and its surroundings.

Follow the detailed steps to extract audio from video/audio archives and edit them in Clipchamp.

mdi-cellphoneStream 2: Record Sounds With Your Cellphone or Field Recorder
Objective: Capture present-day sounds on campus to provide a current perspective using phone or Zoom H6 field recorders.

Follow the detailed steps to record field audio and upload it to your workstations.

mdi-account-voiceStream 3: Voice Annotations or Narrations
Objective: Create audio based on your own narration using the text-to-speech feature in Clipchamp.

Synthesize script readings with subtle background soundscapes for maximum clarity.

mdi-share-variantStep 3: Posting Your Sound Recording for Showcase

Objective: Share your completed audio recording with the class by posting it to our collaborative whiteboard.
